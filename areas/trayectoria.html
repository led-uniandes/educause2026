Trayectoria profesional

El tema 2 del Top 10 pide preparar a los estudiantes con habilidades de pensamiento durables y transferibles, y eso toca de lleno a Trayectoria profesional. Las sesiones mostraron una brecha entre universidades que restringen la IA en los exámenes y empleadores que la exigen, y destacaron el juicio, la verificación y la capacidad de explicar decisiones como lo que más se valora en el trabajo. El empleo estudiantil en servicios de la propia universidad apareció como una vía concreta de experiencia profesional.

Qué vimos
  • Trayectoria profesional tuvo una presencia modesta (24 sesiones relacionadas), concentrada en empleabilidad, IA y empleo estudiantil.
  • Boston University encontró que los asistentes comerciales de carrera dieron respuestas menos profundas que los chatbots generales ante la consulta de un estudiante de primera generación.
  • University of Texas at San Antonio mide la trayectoria temprana de sus egresados con perfiles públicos de LinkedIn y organizó sesiones de escucha con empleadores sobre lo que esperan del uso de IA.
  • En University of Wyoming el 50 % del personal de TI son estudiantes, y Northeastern University señaló que los empleos que piden habilidades de IA pagan una prima del 28 %.
Por qué le importa al área

El tema 2 del Top 10 pide preparar a los estudiantes con habilidades de pensamiento durables y transferibles, y eso toca de lleno a Trayectoria profesional. Las sesiones mostraron una brecha entre universidades que restringen la IA en los exámenes y empleadores que la exigen, y destacaron el juicio, la verificación y la capacidad de explicar decisiones como lo que más se valora en el trabajo. El empleo estudiantil en servicios de la propia universidad apareció como una vía concreta de experiencia profesional.

Recomendaciones

  1. Validar con empleadores colombianos qué competencias de uso de IA esperan de los egresados, con sesiones de escucha estructuradas como las de University of Texas at San Antonio.
  2. Auditar por grupo de estudiantes cualquier herramienta de orientación de carrera con IA antes de contratarla.
  3. Convertir el empleo estudiantil en TI, el LED y otros servicios en una ruta de experiencia profesional documentada, con niveles y funciones por escrito.

Building Student AI Innovation through Entrepreneurship: A Replicable Model for Higher Education

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
Florida State University
  • Poster for Presentation
Descripción

Higher education leaders are under increasing pressure to prepare students for an AI-driven future, yet many institutions struggle to design scalable and inclusive learning experiences. This session presents a proven model for a 48-hour AI hackathon developed through a partnership between a public research university and a cloud provider. The AI Maker Challenge enabled interdisciplinary student teams to build real-world solutions, including a research grant matching platform, a student productivity system, and a personalized health care application. Several projects are now advancing toward institutional implementation or start-up development, demonstrating the event’s effectiveness as an innovation incubator. Attendees will learn how to structure high-impact partnerships in which the industry partner provides infrastructure, tools, and technical expertise while the university delivers space, logistics, and institutional pathways. The session also introduces a three-tier accessibility framework (no-code, low-code, and full development) that supports participation across skill levels.Participants will leave with a practical playbook for launching similar initiatives, including partnership models, budget frameworks, infrastructure requirements, and post-event support strategies.

Cybersecurity, AI and Computing Learning Pathways: Catalyzing Collaboration and Career Opportunities

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
Slippery Rock University of Pennsylvania
  • Educause Poster 2026 [ponente] Mary Ann Leung
Descripción

The increased integration of High-Performance Computing (HPC), artificial intelligence (AI), cybersecurity, and other emerging technologies necessitates a transformative shift in identifying learning pathways to bridge the skills gap between traditional and modern, automated technological landscapes. Various communities have organized engagement initiatives to enable efficient integration of (HPC), AI, and cybersecurity ecosystems. However, there still exists a need for more regional and national collaborative and structured learning/training resources and activities for upskilling/reskilling the workforce to mitigate gaps. This presentation highlights the insights gained by the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) Pilot Sustainable Research Pathways (SRP) program (designed to connect researchers and project leaders with students and faculty across the U.S) and ACM SigHPC education chapter-coordinated HPC training and education workshops to foster collaboration among practitioners from traditional and emerging fields to explore educational needs in HPC, AI, and cybersecurity. We aim, through discussions and case studies centered on learning pathways, to provide insight into research, training and career opportunities; identify opportunities to support regional and national institutions; and broaden access to computing resources through mentorship and collaboration.

Empowering Student Employees to Utilize Their Own Innovation to Benefit the College for the Future

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
Western Carolina University
  • Empowering Student Employees To Utilize Their Own Innovation To Benefit the College For the Future
Descripción

This presentation shows a model for transforming student employees from task-oriented support staff into active contributors of long-term technical solutions. Although many colleges use student workers for frontline assistance, their roles often remain limited to repetitive troubleshooting, leaving significant talent untapped. Through peer-to-peer training, mentoring, a creative problem-solving mindset, and intentional project ownership, students are empowered to identify operational challenges and implement sustainable improvements. Students experience classroom and lab technology firsthand, so they are uniquely positioned to design solutions that address real needs. They lead projects such as developing custom tools and resources that continue to benefit the college long after they graduate. This approach has produced more efficient and reliable support, streamlined processes, and long-term solutions. Students report increased engagement, confidence, and stronger job placement outcomes due to their experience leading authentic technical projects. By intentionally developing student talent, we have built a highly capable and sustainable technical support resource for our college.

One Institute, Many Campuses: Expanding a Public-Private Data Science Consortium

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
Waukesha County Technical College · University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee · Marquette University
  • One Institute Many Campuses Expanding a PublicPrivate Data Science Consortium
Descripción

This session will take you inside the Northwestern Mutual Data Science Institute (NMDSI), a first-of-it-kind public-private consortium that is expanding to include three new academic institutions: a medical college, an engineering-focused university, and a technical college with an applied AI lab. Since 2018, the NMDSI has championed AI and data science transformation across southeastern Wisconsin, investing in research, new faculty, student scholarships, academic programs and, since fall 2023, over 100 applied AI and data science student research projects. With this expansion, partners now pool distinctive assets - clinical research and health data expertise, high-performance computing and a supercomputer, and hands-on applied AI and workforce training - to build a more inclusive, practice-centered talent pipeline for the region. Attendees will learn how the consortium structures governance, shared resources, and programming across very different institutions, and how this model can inform similar regional collaborations that align industry needs, academic innovation, and community impact.

Student Employee Development Process: Improving IT Operations While Developing Student Professionals

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 4:30 p. m. · 1 material
Brigham Young University Hawaii
  • Student Employee Development Process
Descripción

Our vice president recently instructed our IT staff that "our primary job is not to 'get stuff done.' It is to MENTOR our students to 'live and lead.'" That is a sea change for our IT staff and has forced us to rethink much of how we do our work. We're actively looking for ways to give our students more "real-life" substantive technical and professional experiences to better prepare them for life after graduation. This is hard to do. Our "process" has grown out of research into how others have tried to effect similar change, and our own learned experiences. The SEDP is a step-by-step process to help our team identify tasks and responsibilities that can be shifted to students; training, resources, and accountability we need to get in place; and managing the transition in a way that helps the students to be successful. We learned hard lessons from assigning work to student employees who weren't sufficiently prepared, essentially setting them up for failure. We've also had some great success that has deeply enriched our students' educational experience and improved our department operations. Participants will learn a six-step approach for deliberately shifting meaningful technical and operational work to students - without sacrificing compliance, service quality, or stability - while turning student employment into a true capstone learning experience. We have used this process in IT and other campus departments, so the content is relevant to managers across higher ed.

Using Social Media Data to Measure Student Outcomes and Alumni Career Pathways

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 4:30 p. m. · 1 material
University of Texas at San Antonio
  • Poster
Descripción

As institutions increasingly prioritize demonstrating the long-term value of internships and workforce-readiness initiatives, the ability to effectively track and measure alumni career outcomes has become essential. This session presents a practical, data-driven approach to assessing early career success by leveraging publicly available LinkedIn profiles and other social media sources. By systematically analyzing this information, institutions can identify key indicators such as job titles, industries, geographic mobility, and estimated salary ranges of former interns. Participants will gain insight into how this method can provide a clearer picture of post-program outcomes without requiring extensive alumni surveys or costly data tools. The session will also highlight strategies for organizing and interpreting this data to uncover trends, measure program effectiveness, and benchmark outcomes over time. Additionally, attendees will explore how these insights can be used to strengthen institutional reporting, enhance stakeholder communication, and inform program design. By the end of the session, attendees will leave with actionable ideas for implementing a scalable approach to alumni tracking that supports continuous improvement, aligns with institutional goals, and enables more informed, evidence-based decision-making across departments.

Develop Job-Ready Graduates with Hands-On Experience for the AI-Driven Workforce

Jueves 1 de octubre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
Skillable
  • Develop JobReady Graduates Poster
Descripción

Become known for delivering graduates with proven skills in AI, data, cybersecurity, cloud, DevOps, and more.    Today's employers aren't hiring based on diplomas and GPAs alone; they need confidence that their new hire can do the work. Of the organizations surveyed by NACE, 74% cited experiential learning as the top way students can demonstrate capability in skills-based hiring.     Students pursuing technical roles need time at the keyboard doing real work, with real software and scenarios, solving real problems. AI, Work, and the Redesign Higher Education Can No Longer Delay

Jueves 1 de octubre · 1:00 p. m. · 1 material
Arapahoe Community College
  • AI Work and Higher Ed Redesign Presentation
Descripción

Artificial intelligence is not only changing teaching and learning. It is also changing the work students are preparing to do and exposing how poorly many inherited higher education structures fit both today’s learners and today’s labor market. This session offers a practical framework for institutional redesign, using the community college context as a leading-edge case for how colleges can rethink learner guidance, employer engagement, work-based learning, and credential pathways in response to AI-driven workforce change.

Camera-Ready at the Library: Professional Portraits for Academic and Clinical Careers

Jueves 1 de octubre · 3:15 p. m. · 1 material
Harvard University
  • Camera Ready At The Library Professional Portraits For Academic And Clinical Careers
Descripción

This project began with a single patron’s request for a professional portrait. In response, a small photography studio was created, equipped with high-quality cameras, LED lighting, and a comfortable private space. Patrons schedule a 15-minute session to have their photo taken. Without formal advertising, the service quickly gained popularity through word of mouth. Within 24 hours of announcing the new service, more than 300 requests were submitted. To date, over 6,000 photos have been provided to members of the campus community. This project highlights the library’s role as a provider of practical professional resources, especially during a time of limited budgets in higher education. By offering access to quality media equipment and a free photography service, the studio supports career readiness, professional development, and equitable access. The immediate success of the service has made it a staple of our library offerings. Moving forward, our goal is to scale and streamline the program to ensure sustainability in both cost and labor. Our approach and lessons learned may help other libraries develop similar services and encourage broader conversations about expanding offerings within restrictive budgets. This poster will showcase the studio setup, user feedback, a timeline of service milestones, and future goals and challenges, along with suggestions for scaling the service based on community needs and available resources.
