Educación Continua

Educación Continua enfrenta una demanda de credenciales cortas que muchas instituciones todavía no saben registrar ni certificar bien. Los casos de Denver apuntan a tres condiciones, que son registrar a los participantes y sus resultados en un solo lugar, emitir certificados verificables y diseñar programas que se puedan apilar. La formación en IA que varias universidades ya ofrecen a su propio personal, con cursos asíncronos y consultas cortas, también puede convertirse en oferta hacia otras instituciones.

Qué vimos
  • Educación Continua tuvo poca presencia propia en la agenda (15 sesiones relacionadas, 3 como tema principal), y lo más útil vino de casos vecinos.
  • SUNY presentó una plataforma compartida para registrar matrícula, resultados y credenciales de la educación sin crédito en sus 64 campus.
  • Bowdoin College, 1EdTech y University of Hawaii mostraron cómo hacer credenciales digitales verificables y portables con estándares abiertos.
  • El informe Student Voice de Ellucian, con más de 2.000 estudiantes, indica que el 71 % busca credenciales alternativas o apilables y que solo el 3 % participa en ellas.
Por qué le importa al área

Los casos de Denver apuntan a tres condiciones, que son registrar a los participantes y sus resultados en un solo lugar, emitir certificados verificables y diseñar programas que se puedan apilar. La formación en IA que varias universidades ya ofrecen a su propio personal, con cursos asíncronos y consultas cortas, también puede convertirse en oferta hacia otras instituciones.

Recomendaciones

  1. Unificar en una plataforma el registro de participantes, resultados y certificados de los programas sin crédito, siguiendo el caso de SUNY.
  2. Emitir certificados con firma digital e insignias basadas en estándares abiertos que los empleadores puedan verificar.
  3. Diseñar con DIDACTA y el LED un curso corto de IA para directivos y equipos de otras instituciones, a partir de lo que la Universidad ofrezca a su propio personal.

Cybersecurity, AI and Computing Learning Pathways: Catalyzing Collaboration and Career Opportunities

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
Slippery Rock University of Pennsylvania
  • Educause Poster 2026 [ponente] Mary Ann Leung
Descripción

The increased integration of High-Performance Computing (HPC), artificial intelligence (AI), cybersecurity, and other emerging technologies necessitates a transformative shift in identifying learning pathways to bridge the skills gap between traditional and modern, automated technological landscapes. Various communities have organized engagement initiatives to enable efficient integration of (HPC), AI, and cybersecurity ecosystems. However, there still exists a need for more regional and national collaborative and structured learning/training resources and activities for upskilling/reskilling the workforce to mitigate gaps. This presentation highlights the insights gained by the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) Pilot Sustainable Research Pathways (SRP) program (designed to connect researchers and project leaders with students and faculty across the U.S) and ACM SigHPC education chapter-coordinated HPC training and education workshops to foster collaboration among practitioners from traditional and emerging fields to explore educational needs in HPC, AI, and cybersecurity. We aim, through discussions and case studies centered on learning pathways, to provide insight into research, training and career opportunities; identify opportunities to support regional and national institutions; and broaden access to computing resources through mentorship and collaboration.

Dynamic Pedagogy: Personalized, Skills-Based Learning through Adaptivity and Immersive Environments

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
Skillwell
  • Dynamic Pedagogy Poster
Descripción

The asynchronous, digital education landscape is transitioning from passive content delivery to dynamic learning architectures. To bridge the gap between knowledge acquisition, learning by doing, and the application of key skills and behaviors, this presentation explores two key technologies that, in conjunction, create impactful training with demonstrable results. These technologies are adaptive learner journeys and immersive simulations. One-size-fits-all training or teaching is inefficient, alienating experts while overwhelming novices. Adaptivity solves this by utilizing algorithmic approaches to analyze performance in real time. Instead of a linear path, systems curate bespoke experiences, scaffolding difficulty based on performance data. Every minute spent contributes to growth, keeping cognitive engagement at its peak. Providing personalization at scale, intelligent pathing respects existing knowledge while increasing learning efficiency. If adaptive journeys provide the maps, simulations are the terrain. Training is truly impactful when it is experiential. High-fidelity branching scenarios provide a risk-free environment to fail and iterate. This builds the emotional resonance and "muscle memory" that passive modalities cannot replicate. Students don’t just recall facts; they develop the confidence to apply what they know under pressure. The synergy of data-driven personalization and realistic practice transforms learning into validated competency-based mastery.

The Metaverse Is Dead, Long Live Virtual Worlds! Three European Consortia, One Institute of Technology

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 4:30 p. m. · 1 material
CSIESR
  • EDU26 KOSCIELNIAK ENSAM
Descripción

How does a single engineering school go from building one immersive digital twin platform to co-leading Europe's flagship skills academy for virtual worlds in under five years? Arts et Métiers Institute of Technology - ENSAM traces that trajectory through three consortia, each a deliberate step up in scale: JENII, a nationally funded proof of concept; IDT4EU, an Erasmus+ partnership with Warsaw University of Technology, Politecnico di Milano and Finnish SME New Education Horizon testing whether the approach transfers; and ViWAS, the CYENS - coordinated Virtual Worlds Academy of Skills, now building the competence framework for immersive technologies across the continent. Beyond the Webcam: Building Instructor Presence without Video in Asynchronous Online Courses

Jueves 1 de octubre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
University of South Florida St. Petersburg · University of South Florida
  • Beyond the Webcam Poster
Descripción

Camera shy, privacy-conscious, or simply camera-free? You don't need video to build meaningful instructor presence. E-learning literature and research strongly suggest that online instructors should build their presence into a successful asynchronous course. However, many faculty often assume that replicating in-person presence requires a talking-head video in every module. This poster presentation will challenge the talking-head video assumption by highlighting five evidence-based strategies for building authentic instructor presence in asynchronous courses without video. From Unconnected to Unified: A System-Wide Student Platform for 64 Campuses

Jueves 1 de octubre · 2:15 p. m.
SUNY System Administration
Descripción

The State University of New York (SUNY) System faced a critical information gap in measuring and managing one of its fastest growing areas: non-credit education. Inability to track enrollment, outcomes, or credentials for non-credit learners left the SUNY System strategically vulnerable in the face of fast approaching federal policy changes like Workforce Pell. To meet this challenge, SUNY’s technology leaders flipped the script, turning a data-management issue in one of its most fragmented areas into an opportunity to assert SUNY’s competitiveness on the national stage. As part of SUNY’s Digital Transformation Project, the System set out to scope a Non-Credit Student Information Management (NCSIM) initiative, initiating a multi-year effort to implement a cloud-based platform providing 64 individually operated campuses with shared infrastructure for non-credit registration, tracking, credentialing, and marketing. What emerged was a blueprint for a shared, learner-focused platform that unified all of SUNY’s offerings, both non-credit and for-credit, fundamentally altering the value proposition of New York’s public university system. Once complete, the platform will provide prospective and current students access to the combined value of the SUNY System - 64 campuses, 32,000+ faculty, and 7,000+ programs - through a single interface, expanding upon SUNY’s position as a provider of career-boosting credentials and offering students pathways into matriculating degree programs.

Beyond the Badge: Creating Interoperable Credential Ecosystems That Work

Viernes 2 de octubre · 8:00 a. m. · 1 material
Bowdoin College · 1EdTech · University of Hawaii System Office
  • Presentation Beyond the Badge
Descripción

Digital credentials are rapidly expanding across higher education, yet many institutions increasingly confront a growing challenge: fragmentation. Learners now earn badges, certificates, and microcredentials. But too often, these credentials lack portability, verifiability, and clear employer recognition. This panel will show how open standards and interoperable frameworks transform digital credentials from isolated achievements into trusted signals of skills and learning. Leveraging interoperability standards and real-world implementations, panelists will explain how institutions can design credential ecosystems that support portability across platforms, enable employer verification, and align with workforce needs. Attendees will discover how standards-based approaches lower complexity, build trust, and maximize the value of digital credentials for learners, institutions, and employers.
