Decanatura de Estudiantes

La Decanatura recibe los efectos de la IA antes de que lleguen a las políticas, en el bienestar, la carga y la manera en que los estudiantes piden ayuda. La conferencia mostró que los asistentes y las alertas tempranas dan resultado cuando hay un responsable humano y un flujo de intervención claro. También mostró que las herramientas compradas pueden atender peor a estudiantes de primera generación o de grupos subrepresentados, y eso obliga a probarlas por grupo antes de adoptarlas.

Qué vimos
  • Los asistentes para estudiantes pasaron de responder preguntas frecuentes a conocer la situación de cada estudiante; University of South Carolina y California State University, San Bernardino los autentican y los conectan con asesores humanos.
  • University of Wisconsin-Superior revisa de forma central unas 1.400 alertas tempranas y lanza campañas de citas, con entre 12 y 17 % más persistencia en quienes recibieron apoyo.
  • Boston University probó seis herramientas de IA con la consulta de un estudiante LGBTQIA+ de primera generación, y los asistentes de carrera comerciales dieron las respuestas menos profundas.
  • Colorado Mountain College recordó que al menos el 10 % de los estudiantes tiene un diagnóstico neurocognitivo documentado, y Denison University exigió a 722 estudiantes de primer ingreso un módulo digital de unos 50 minutos.
Por qué le importa al área

La Decanatura recibe los efectos de la IA antes de que lleguen a las políticas, en el bienestar, la carga y la manera en que los estudiantes piden ayuda. La conferencia mostró que los asistentes y las alertas tempranas dan resultado cuando hay un responsable humano y un flujo de intervención claro. También mostró que las herramientas compradas pueden atender peor a estudiantes de primera generación o de grupos subrepresentados, y eso obliga a probarlas por grupo antes de adoptarlas.

Recomendaciones

  1. Antes de adoptar un asistente o un sistema de alertas, definir qué datos lee, cuándo interviene una persona y quién responde por cada alerta.
  2. Probar con perfiles de estudiantes reales cualquier herramienta de acompañamiento comprada y comparar la calidad de las respuestas por grupo.
  3. Incorporar en la inducción un módulo corto de habilidades digitales e IA que tomen todos los estudiantes de primer semestre.

Built, Not Bought: Delivering a Fast, Frugal, and Fully Accessible Virtual Safety Walk Mobile App

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
University of Houston
  • Built Not Bought Poster
Descripción

In today’s evolving campus environment, students expect fast, mobile-first services. At the University of Houston (UH), we saw an opportunity to meet these needs by leveraging creative technology solutions where we could deliver a safety-focused app with custom features. With limited resources and a growing need for scalable solutions, we partnered with the University of Houston Police Department (UHPD) to build the UHPD app featuring the Virtual Safety Walk. This digital alternative to traditional escorts allows students to request a virtual safety walk directly from their remotely monitored phones, eliminating the wait for an officer to be dispatched, optimizing the use of valuable personnel, and saving time in students' busy schedules. Using UH property lines (geofencing) and real-time location tracking, UHPD can virtually monitor a student’s walk and respond quickly as needed, greatly improving response time and campus coverage. Originally designed to be provided in UH Go, the UH official mobile app, the university pivoted to a standalone app as part of our ecosystem to meet the needs of users and UHPD. Designed with student habits in mind, the app is fast, intuitive, and available 24/7. It empowers users with control and convenience while enhancing campus safety through smarter, tech-driven solutions. Come to this session and learn more.

Supporting Students with Disabilities in Online Higher Education: Bridging the Transition Gap

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
National University
  • Resources for Students with Disabilities in Online Higher Ed poster session
Descripción

As online learning continues to expand in higher education, students with disabilities often face significant challenges navigating support services and accommodations. Many students transition from secondary environments, where services are mandated under IDEA, to postsecondary settings where they must self-advocate and independently access resources. This shift creates a crucial gap that can impact student success.This session is designed for faculty teaching in online environments who want to better support students with disabilities. Participants will examine the transition from secondary to postsecondary education and explore how differences in service models affect student access and equity. The session will highlight the barriers students encounter and the role faculty can play to address them. Through guided student scenarios and discussion, participants will identify strategies to recognize when students may need additional support, apply inclusive practices within their courses, and connect students to institutional services. Participants will also develop a course of action tailored to their own teaching context, including modifying syllabus language, creating communication plans, and establishing referral pathways. By the end of the session, faculty will be better equipped to support student success in online higher education environments.

High School to the Hill: Integrating Digital Foundations into First-Year Orientation

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 3:30 p. m. · 1 material
Denison University
  • High School to the Hill Integrating Digital Foundations into FirstYear Orientation
Descripción

In recent years, faculty have observed that students often lack essential tech skills in areas such as file management and storage, digital library resources, cybersecurity, operating systems, and troubleshooting. These gaps create unnecessary stress as students begin their academic journeys, and can detract from valuable class time. Denison’s Educational Technology team partnered with Cybersecurity, the Library, Enrollment, Fraternity and Sorority Life, and First-Year Experience to address these skill gaps through asynchronous Canvas modules. Designed as a bridge between incoming students’ high school tech skills and the competencies expected in college, our approach encourages reflection on past practices, includes practical hands-on activities, and introduces students to our campus culture. The design was informed by faculty insights, in-class observations, surveys of current first-years, and feedback from students involved in fraternities and sororities. Incoming students completed the modules in July 2026 as part of their onboarding prior to moving to campus. Our presentation will outline our planning, data collection, course design, coordination with campus partners, and reflections/lessons learned on the outcomes of this pilot initiative. Participants will have opportunities to brainstorm how to implement similar initiatives at their institutions, including identifying areas for digital skill-strengthening, potential collaborators, and developing actionable next steps.

An AI-Enabled Wellbeing and Advising Framework: From Fragmented Data to Coordinated Student Support

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 4:30 p. m. · 1 material
Tecnologico de Monterrey
  • Not just chatbots a network of specialized agents orchestrators and humans in the loop so 500 1
Descripción

This session presents the Wellbeing & Advising Framework (WAF), an institutional program at Tecnológico de Monterrey that leverages artificial intelligence, learning analytics, and coordinated support systems to transform student success across the academic lifecycle - from admission to graduation. WAF addresses a persistent challenge in higher education: fragmented data and disconnected support services that delay timely, personalized interventions. This session offers an honest narrative of what it takes to design, pilot, and scale AI infrastructure within a complex, multi-campus institution - not a finished product, but a replicable model in motion. The framework combines four key capabilities: real-time risk detection through early-alert intelligence; holistic student profiles integrating academic, behavioral, and wellbeing data; an AI-powered policy assistant (TECbot) that translates complex regulations into actionable guidance; and a personalization engine connecting students to co-curricular opportunities aligned to their trajectory. Early results show strong adoption: over 40% of program directors and advisors actively using the platform and a satisfaction score of 4.83 out of 5. Participants will gain practical insight into designing a human-centered, AI-enabled student support ecosystem - enabling 500+ mentors and advisors across 26 campuses to move from reactive counseling to proactive, coordinated, and personalized student success strategies.

Turning Early Alerts into Impact: A Coordinated, Data-Informed Model for Student Success

Jueves 1 de octubre · 9:30 a. m. · 2 materiales
University of Wisconsin-Superior
  • Turning Early Alerts Into Impact Implementation Toolkit
  • Turning Alerts into Impact
Descripción

Institutions increasingly rely on early alerts and predictive analytics to support student success, but without clear ownership and coordinated workflows, alerts often fail to translate into meaningful action. This session presents a scalable, campaign-based model for converting academic alerts into completed interventions that improve persistence and demonstrate institutional impact .Using a campus case study, presenters will share how clearly defined alert ownership, role-routed outreach, and automated appointment campaigns reduced intervention friction, protected staff capacity, and led to measurable gains in student persistence. The session will also explore how persistence outcomes were translated into maintained tuition revenue to support strategic decision-making and leadership buy-in. Participants will leave with adaptable design principles to build alert-to-action systems that align teaching and learning goals with institutional strategy, whether they use Navigate360 or other student success platforms.
