Gestión académica

La Gestión académica depende de datos confiables y procesos claros, y la IA amplifica cualquier desorden en ambos. Los casos de Denver muestran que el gobierno de datos es ante todo gestión del cambio, con dominios, responsables y definiciones compartidas. Sobre esa base, la analítica con IA ya llega a manos de directivos, con acceso acotado y validación de la oficina de datos.

Qué vimos
  • Hartnell College usa una sola plataforma de datos para reportes, integraciones y detección de fraude, y Southeastern University bajó un reporte del sistema académico de 3 días a 5 minutos con consultas en lenguaje natural y control de roles.
  • En Cal Poly Humboldt, un análisis de encuesta estimado en más de 80 horas de analista pasó a un borrador de unas 26 páginas hecho con IA en cerca de una hora, seguido de un día de revisión humana.
  • Jagiellonian University adoptó el sello electrónico cualificado y bajó la emisión de documentos de un día a 5 minutos, con 10.422 documentos sellados en un año.
  • Virginia Tech reemplaza más de 1.900 personalizaciones de su sistema de información estudiantil, y Minnesota State evitó construir 60.000 pies cuadrados gracias a datos de uso de espacios.
Por qué le importa al área

Sobre esa base, la analítica con IA ya llega a manos de directivos, con acceso acotado y validación de la oficina de datos.

Recomendaciones

  1. Formalizar roles de líder de dominio, administrador y custodio de datos, y acordar definiciones institucionales como estudiante activo o matriculado.
  2. Pilotear analítica de autoservicio con IA sobre una sola decisión real, con un directivo responsable y validación de la oficina de datos.
  3. Revisar la firma y el sellado digital de certificados y constancias para reducir los tiempos de emisión.

The Pilot That Became the Playbook: How ULM Rebuilt Institutional Reporting from the Ground Up

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 2:15 p. m. · 3 materiales
K16 Solutions · University of Louisiana at Monroe · University of Louisiana System Office
  • Speakers
  • DataX Twopager overview
  • Scaffold DataX Comprehensive Guide
Descripción

When the University of Louisiana System set out to standardize reporting across campuses that had never standardized on the same stack, it started with one pilot. ULM spent the past year rebuilding its data governance and reporting foundation: completers, census, applicant funnel, and a cash-reporting blueprint, with the goal of producing something other institutions within the system could adopt, rather than reinvent. This session brings together the campus IT leader who reconciled decades of organically evolved systems; the system leader who had to align governance expectations across institutions; and the product leader who had to build for both at once. From Isolated to Unified: Building Trustworthy AI on Decision-Grade Institutional Data (a Hybrid Classroom Experience)

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 3:30 p. m. · 1 material
Carnegie Mellon University
  • Waddell and Zheng Building Trustworthy AI on Decision Grade Enterprise Data
Descripción

Artificial intelligence is only as trustworthy as the data that powers it. As institutions accelerate AI adoption, many are discovering that traditional data governance alone is insufficient to support responsible and reliable AI deployment. This session explores how Carnegie Mellon University integrated data governance and AI governance into a unified operational framework designed to enable trustworthy AI. We will discuss how aligning governance structures across data stewardship, analytics, and AI initiatives creates the foundation for reliable natural language interfaces, reduced hallucination risk, and scalable institutional AI innovation. Drawing on higher education practice and emerging governance guidance, the presenters will introduce a practical framework for transforming distributed institutional data into AI-ready data products. Participants will learn how establishing data maturity standards, audited data pipelines, and SQL-augmented AI architectures enables accurate natural language interrogation of institutional data. Attendees will leave with a higher ed-tailored blueprint for moving from siloed data management to unified data and AI governance, enabling trustworthy analytics, natural language access to institutional data, and safer deployment of AI workflows and agents.

Counting What Counts: A Research Paradigm for Institutional Equity Accountability

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 4:30 p. m. · 1 material
University of Georgia
  • Counting What Counts LibQuant Paradigm
Descripción

Critical race scholarship established that quantitative data is never neutral - it carries the assumptions of the structures that produced it. But naming that problem is not a methodology. This session introduces LiberationQuant, an extension of the QuantCrit research paradigm built on three operational pillars - Triggers, Tracking, and Treatment - that move institutions from critique to accountability. Attendees will leave with a methodological framework for repositioning the institution, rather than the student, as the primary unit of quantitative equity inquiry.

Democratizing Data: Transforming SIS Reporting with Natural Language AI

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 4:30 p. m. · 1 material
Southeastern University · Jenzabar
  • Ask Analyze Act AI removes the technical bottleneck empowering users to generate reports them
Descripción

What if every department on campus could access the data they need, without submitting a ticket, waiting for IT, or learning SQL? In this session, discover how Natural Language-to-SQL AI is transforming reporting across the Student Information System (SIS) and accelerating institutional insights. The solution, designed specifically for nontechnical staff, empowers users to ask complex questions in plain English - such as enrollment trends, financial summaries, retention metrics, or program performance - and instantly receive accurate, permission-based reports. Attendees will learn how AI securely translates natural language into optimized SQL queries across a complex SIS database, eliminating reporting bottlenecks while maintaining data governance and role-based security. The result: faster insights, reduced IT dependency, and a more data-informed campus culture. Join us to explore real-world use cases across admissions, finance, student success, and academic affairs. Learn how institutions are accelerating decision-making by putting powerful analytics directly into the hands of those who need it most.

Process with Purpose: Building an Enterprise Business Process Analysis Team

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 4:30 p. m. · 1 material
University of North Carolina, Charlotte
  • Process with Purpose Building an Enterprise Business Process Analysis Team
Descripción

Have you ever launched a major technology project, only to discover that the underlying business process was broken? Many higher education IT departments are consistently tasked with implementing enterprise software to solve deeply rooted operational inefficiencies. This often results in heavy customizations, ballooning budgets, and frustrated end users. To combat this, we expanded our traditional Project Management Office into a holistic department of Planning, Projects, and Process Strategy by building a Business Process Analysis (BPA) team from the ground up. In this session, we will explore why we needed a dedicated BPA function to serve as neutral facilitators across the institution. We will share how we structured the team, secured executive buy-in, and established our standard operating procedures - from intake and statements of work to producing actionable Business Process Requirements Documents (BPRDs). Finally, we will provide a portfolio overview of our first year, highlighting how our embedded analysts successfully supported 18 cross-functional initiatives across academic and administrative divisions. Attendees will learn how prioritizing process over technology clarifies scope, breaks down departmental silos, and drastically reduces implementation risks before any software is procured.

Building a Dashboard Ecosystem Leaders Actually Use

Jueves 1 de octubre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
Colorado State University-Pueblo
  • Dashboard Ecosystem Poster
Descripción

Higher education leaders are increasingly asked to make high-stakes decisions with incomplete, fragmented, or inconsistently framed data. This session explores how CSU Pueblo has approached this challenge by developing an evolving dashboard ecosystem that supports institutional sense-making, prioritization, and evidence-based action across enrollment management, extended studies, financial aid, and academic planning. Rather than focus on individual dashboards, the presenters examine how a coordinated analytics environment helps leaders develop a shared understanding of institutional conditions, surface potential risks and opportunities, and evaluate programmatic impact. Examples include identifying emerging trends and creating concise, visually rich executive summaries. These views enable leaders to justify programming and resource decisions by clearly connecting enrollment patterns, academic offerings, and financial considerations. The session also highlights the importance of user-centered design and sustained collaboration between analytics staff and executive leaders. Through iterative dialogue, dashboards are shaped to emphasize clarity, relevance, and usability, ensuring they remain trusted tools rather than static reports. Participants will gain practical insights into designing dashboard ecosystems that adapt as questions change, support meaningful strategic conversations, and foster confidence in institutional decision-making.
