Admisiones

Admisiones es la puerta por donde entran los aspirantes reales y también los intentos de fraude, y los datos de esa etapa alimentan todo lo que sigue. Hubo pocas sesiones dedicadas al área (26 relacionadas), casi todas sobre fraude, asistentes conversacionales y analítica de mercadeo. En Estados Unidos el fraude apunta a las ayudas federales y en Colombia tendría otra forma, pero la verificación graduada por riesgo y la revisión previa al ingreso del registro al sistema son prácticas aplicables aquí.

Qué vimos
  • El fraude con estudiantes fantasma, que usan identidades robadas o sintéticas, fue el tema más repetido; University of Maryland Global Campus y Accenture estiman pérdidas anuales de cientos de millones de dólares en Estados Unidos.
  • San Jose State University revisa cada noche las solicitudes con 22 indicadores explicables antes de que entren al SIS; en un archivo de 988 solicitudes, las 89 de alto riesgo pertenecían a un mismo grupo.
  • Western Governors University resuelve por completo el 64 % de los chats de su sitio, que recibe unos 11 millones de visitas al mes, y University of Louisville bajó la evaluación de créditos de transferencia de dos semanas a dos minutos.
  • El tema 8 del Top 10 pide combinar datos del SIS, el LMS y otras fuentes para entender los cambios en la matrícula, como hace California Lutheran University desde la solicitud hasta el grado.

Recomendaciones

  1. Definir con la Dirección de TI indicadores de solicitudes sospechosas y revisarlas, con una persona a cargo, antes de que el registro entre al sistema académico.
  2. Medir cualquier asistente para aspirantes por resolución completa y paso a una persona, y declarar siempre que es automatizado.
  3. Conectar los datos de mercadeo, inscripción y desempeño académico para explicar con las facultades qué hay detrás de los cambios en la matrícula.

Conversational AI: How WGU Shifts Gears to Reinvent Student Enrollment Outreach

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
Western Governors University · EDMO
  • Conversational AI How WGU Shifts Gears
Descripción

At many institutions, AI is shifting from a support tool to an active participant in enrollment, capable of taking the next step on a student's behalf, not just responding to one. As expectations for fast, personalized guidance grow, institutions are rethinking how enrollment itself is designed and run. This session explores how Western Governors University is evolving its enrollment model by adopting agentic AI that acts autonomously across the applicant journey, from inquiry through matriculation. Rather than only building these capabilities in-house, WGU has focused on also selecting agentic technologies that align with its operational needs and enrollment goals. The future vision for this technology includes systems that handle routine coordination, sequence follow-ups, and guide students through key steps, freeing teams to focus on moments that matter most. The session will highlight how agentic AI integrates into existing workflows, reshapes team structure, and complements human judgment. It will also cover vendor selection, implementation challenges, and ensuring autonomous actions stay accurate and student-friendly. Attendees will leave understanding how agentic AI is reinventing enrollment as a whole, not just outreach.

Georgia State University
Descripción

As the landscape of higher education demands more specialized offerings, academic units require increasingly nuanced support from administrative systems. Historically, technical limitations have often dictated academic policy, creating a rigid system design that acts as a barrier to effective collaboration. At the Georgia State University Graduate School, we recognized this hurdle and moved beyond the “standardization vs. customization” binary to build a culture of institutional agility. This session explores how to transform the perception of a central administrative office from a gatekeeper to a strategic partner. We will demonstrate how to shield the student experience from back-end complexity, ensuring that applicants receive clear and accurate information while maintaining administrative integrity. Join us to discover how to lead with a “yes” and utilize technology to handle extreme program variation with ease.

Ask the Expert: Beyond Ghost Students: Building a Cross-Campus Response to Identity and Financial Fraud

Miércoles 30 de septiembre · 10:45 a. m.
University of Montana
Descripción

Ghost student cases have revealed a challenge that extends far beyond traditional financial aid fraud. Institutions are encountering student accounts used to launder money rather than pay tuition, fraud indicators scattered across multiple offices without coordination, and identity verification processes that were never designed to withstand sophisticated, well-funded adversaries. This session is designed as a facilitated discussion rather than a formal presentation. Participants will explore what their institutions are seeing, the risks that concern them most, and how campuses are addressing, or struggling to address, these evolving threats. The discussion leader will share lessons learned from a year of investigating ghost student cases, including efforts to aggregate data across enrollment and financial systems, as well as unresolved challenges that remain. Attendees are encouraged to bring their own experiences, questions, and complex cases for a candid peer exchange.

Measuring Chatbot Impact in Higher Ed: Measuring Chatbot Success and Performance Gaps

Jueves 1 de octubre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
University of Houston
  • Measuring Chatbot Impact in Higher Ed Measuring Chatbot Success and Performance Gaps
Descripción

As chatbot adoption accelerates across higher education, institutions face a critical question: How do we measure success beyond basic usage metrics? This session presents a practical framework for evaluating chatbot performance using real institutional data from a scaled deployment that grew from 4 enrollment-focused chatbots to 17 across campus services. Participants will explore how metrics such as resolution rate, deflection rate, conversation volume, and staff engagement provide a multidimensional view of chatbot effectiveness. This framework separates signals of success such as high-resolution rates and strong staff engagement from indicators of performance gaps, including escalations and low-performing topics, enabling teams to identify gaps and prioritize improvements. We will also examine how chatbot data informs integration decisions that drive more personalized user experiences, as well as how combining chatbot analytics with call center data reveals true service impact. Attendees will leave with actionable strategies to measure, interpret, and improve chatbot performance at scale.

Sealing the Future: Digitizing University Documents with the Qualified Electronic Seal

Jueves 1 de octubre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
Jagiellonian University
  • POSTER Sealing The Future
Descripción

In the era of progressive digitization, universities face new challenges. The question no longer is whether to digitize document workflows, but how to do it in a way that is legally compliant and works across institutional and national borders. Our poster presents the implementation of the qualified electronic seal at Jagiellonian University in Kraków, Poland. This solution is based on the European Union’s eIDAS Regulation, which provides a legal framework for electronic trust services across the EU. This regulation has significantly advanced digital procedures not only for universities, but for all institutions in the public and private sectors. The electronic seal has transformed how official student documents are issued. We implemented a qualified electronic seal that can be easily applied to PDF files. It works with the main Electronic Document Management system used at the university, making it part of everyday administrative work. Authorized staff members can digitally sign documents, making them official, legally valid, and protected against any changes. Signed documents are recognized across all EU countries without the need for printing paper, scanning, or physical presence. Our case study shows how legal frameworks and international standards can accelerate digitization in ways that technical solutions alone cannot achieve. The outcomes of our project: faster certificate issuance, reduced paper usage, and a fully digital workflow that is legal across an entire continent.

Stopping Fraud at the Front Door: An AI Screening Model Before SIS Entry

Jueves 1 de octubre · 9:30 a. m. · 1 material
San Jose State University
  • Catching Student Application Fraud
Descripción

Fraudulent and suspicious admissions applications are creating growing challenges for colleges and universities. These include manual cleanup, data quality issues, and downstream risk once bad records enter core campus systems. Many institutions still address this problem too late, after records have already reached the student information system. This session shares a practical upstream approach to screening suspicious applications before they move into the SIS. The model combines AI-assisted scoring, duplicate identity detection, batch-pattern analysis, and human review to identify records that may require closer examination. Rather than replacing existing admissions workflows, it adds a focused and explainable checkpoint that works alongside current enterprise processes. Attendees will gain a replicable framework for designing an AI-supported screening process that helps reduce manual vetting, improves data integrity, and strengthens operational controls. The session will highlight where institutions can intervene in the data flow, what kinds of fraud indicators to evaluate, how to structure confidence-based review, and how to measure whether the process is improving both efficiency and accuracy over time.
